# Adoption playbook: designing an innovation unit

> A practical checklist derived from `operating-model-lessons.md` (lesson IDs in brackets). The underlying evidence is mostly moderate-to-weak and unverified against primary pages (see `../METHODOLOGY.md`). Use this as a set of hypotheses to test, not as proven best practice.

## 1. Before you start: conditions check

Ask honestly whether you have what the successful centers had:
- A **funding base** that survives a bad quarter, or a clear plan for the funding logic you will use instead [L1, L4].
- A **named sponsor** at executive level with authority over the budget [L1].
- A **first customer inside the business**, or an explicit commercialization route [L14, L15].
- If you lack these, the lab will likely follow PARC or the post-1984 Bell Labs pattern [L14, L19].

## 2. Design choices

| Decision | Options from the evidence | Pick when |
|---|---|---|
| Mandate | Mission with a date (Skunk Works, Apollo) vs. open exploration (Bell Labs, HHMI) | Date-bound need → mission; unknown problem space → long-horizon people funding [L2, L5] |
| Authority | One empowered leader or program manager | Always; pair with admin flexibility [L3] |
| Team | Small, controlled outside contact; flat structure | Early-stage work; add integration function when scaling (Apollo) [L9, Mission lesson 3] |
| Selection | Heilmeier-style questions; riskiest-assumption-first; cheap kills | Every project [L5, L6] |
| Hedging | Parallel path for the single point of failure | Where one technical assumption could sink the program [L7] |
| Exit route | Internal transfer, licensing, spin-out with outside CEO/VC governance | Decide **before** the invention matures [L14, L16] |
| Governance | Fixed lifespan with milestone gates; review points | New or unproven units [Institutes lesson 4, weak] |

## 3. Operating rhythm

1. **Intake:** require answers to Heilmeier-style questions and interim milestones for each project [L5].
2. **Early test:** attack the riskiest assumption first on a small budget [L6].
3. **Kill or continue** at gates; make stopping a normal, rewarded outcome (X says it does this; the details are self-reported) [L6].
4. **Hedge** single points of failure early [L7].
5. **Transition plan** owned by a named person from the start; track outcomes with pre-registered metrics, not only self-reported counts [L14, DARPA lesson 6].
6. **Portfolio review** on power-law logic rather than average return (inference) [L8].

## 4. Anti-patterns seen repeatedly

- Judging new ideas only by the parent's current business model [PARC].
- Letting ownership or budget shocks hit research without a protected mandate [L19].
- Copying surface features (isolation, a kill rate, 20% time) without the funding and authority conditions [L4, L22].
- Crediting the model with outcomes the evidence only partly supports (DARPA/Moderna, DARPA/Siri, PARC/Apple myth) [L22].
- Isolating a unit with no reintegration plan [L18].

## 5. Measuring whether it works

The profiles found little robust outcome measurement. Reasonable starting metrics, flagged as this knowledge base's inference: share of projects transitioned to a business owner, follow-on funding or revenue, time from start to first customer test, kill rate with documented reasons, and retention of key people.
