# Cross-cutting operating-model lessons

> **Provenance.** This file synthesizes the profiles in `../centers/` and `research-literature-on-rd-organization.md`. The profiles were re-checked against opened pages in a second pass (see `../METHODOLOGY.md`); each profile's header lists what was opened and what stayed unverified, and carries the numbered source list. Citations here name the profile, not a source number, because the source lists were renumbered in that pass. Grades describe the strength of the evidence found, not certainty about every fact.

Evidence key: **strong** = quasi-experimental or multiple independent sources; **moderate** = well-documented, consistent across sources, causal weight not isolated; **weak** = single case or anecdote; **inference** = this knowledge base's judgment.

## 1. Funding and mandate

**L1. Fund from a stable, mission-tied base, with an explicit sponsor.** [moderate]
- Bell Labs' freedom is linked to monopoly income. The funding was a small share of the Bell System's revenue (sources give about 3 cents per toll-call dollar, or about a tenth of the phone bill), so the lesson is stability, not size [Bell Labs]. Five of its ten Nobel Prizes (Nokia's count) came from work after the 1982 divestiture, so decline was not instant.
- GE and DuPont's fundamental research needed executive sponsorship and a protected budget; Stine's mandate is documented (Dec 1926 memo, $25,000/month, 25 chemists). Mowery shows the labs also absorbed outside technology, and DuPont got many innovations externally. Nylon is the exception [Industrial labs].
- Google X's long bets face budget-cycle pressure (Everyday Robots shut Jan 2023; Intrinsic folded into Google, Feb 2026) [Google X].
- Janelia/HHMI-style long horizons have the best-quantified evidence, but it concerns HHMI investigators, not campuses (see L2) [Institutes].

**L2. Fund people and long horizons when the goal is exploration.** [moderate-to-strong] HHMI vs. NIH: HHMI investigators show about +39% publications, +55% in the top 5% and about +97% in the top 1% (69 HHMI vs. 348 prize-winner controls; selection on observables, not randomized). The effect is concentrated in the upper tail [Literature; Institutes]. Fraunhofer's own site does not support a "success-linked base funding" mechanism: base funding is capped at about a third, contract research is 88% [Institutes].

**L3. Pair decision authority with administrative flexibility.** [moderate] DARPA's program-manager authority works alongside Other Transaction authority (first granted 1989; now 10 USC 4021-4022) and special hiring authority (1998; cap raised from 100 to 140 in 2019) [DARPA]. Skunk Works shows the same pattern: Rules 1, 11 and 12 make one accountable leader, timely funding and trust explicit preconditions [Skunk Works].

**L4. Replace the funding logic explicitly if you lack the original conditions.** [inference] Bell Labs' monopoly, Skunk Works' classified sole-sponsor context and mission labs' wartime urgency are conditions most firms lack. The 1956 decree finding (L17) shows the lab's effect was strongest outside telecom, so the pure-research case is weaker than the legend [Bell Labs; Mission labs].

## 2. Selection and process

**L5. Force clarity up front.** [weak-to-moderate] Heilmeier-style questions and milestones at DARPA program creation; Skunk Works' named customer and fixed date; Apollo's concrete mission. The Heilmeier catechism (seven questions on the Caltech page) and Stage-Gate are practitioner heuristics with little outcome evidence [DARPA; Skunk Works; Literature]. Caveat: the CIA's own history says the A-12 ran about a year late and well over cost, so "speed against targets" is not an unqualified result [Skunk Works].

**L6. Test the riskiest assumption first and make killing cheap.** [weak-to-moderate, self-reported] X's Operating Manual confirms kill criteria set in advance and stage gates, but describes no bonus for killing. The widely cited "98% killed early" figure appears only in a podcast summary, not as a Teller quote, and is unsupported; TechCrunch (Oct 2025) gives about a 2% hit rate and 44% of spend going to graduates [Google X]. Skunk Works' staged proof (Have Blue before F-117) is the defense-industry analogue [Skunk Works].

**L7. Run cheap parallel hedges where one assumption could fail.** [moderate, qualified] Los Alamos' implosion work began early (E-5 group, early 1943); Pu-240 was found in April 1944, and implosion was approved in March 1945. It was scaled up late rather than funded as a 50/50 parallel path from the start. Los Alamos was about 3-4% of Manhattan spend; the sources disagree and are unreconciled [Mission labs].

**L8. Judge a portfolio on power-law outcomes.** [weak/inference] Inferred from Waymo's valuation ($126B) against Other Bets losses (10-K: $4.64B 2022, $4.10B 2023, $4.44B 2024, $7.52B 2025, of which $2.1B is a Waymo compensation charge); no source computes a net portfolio return, and per-project losses are not disclosed [Google X].

**L8a. Own the work end to end.** [weak] Skunk Works' programs show one team owning design, build and test; the one copycat study (TechPlus, by David Oliver) found all but one of the staff later left [Skunk Works].

## 3. Structure, people and culture

**L9. Small, empowered teams with controlled outside interference.** [moderate for design, weak for causation] Skunk Works' 14 rules are now confirmed verbatim from Lockheed's PDF, but "size caused success" is anecdotal, and the rules' dating (1943 start, formalized early 1950s) rests on secondary copies. Literature: small teams tend to disrupt, large teams develop (Wu, Wang & Evans), with a contested measurement [Skunk Works; Literature].

**L10. Build a research community by recruiting through an elite network.** [strong only for ARPA-network recruiting] PARC's Computer Science Lab was built through Bob Taylor's ARPA/SRI network. Claims about "heavy SRI recruiting" and "43 ex-SRI staff" were removed as unsupported [Xerox PARC].

**L11. Co-location helps, but do not over-credit architecture.** [weak] Gertner stresses proximity; Owen-Smith finds mixed causal support. The Allen-curve 50 m figure is hard to trace [Bell Labs; Literature].

**L12. Compartmentalize outward, share inward.** [weak] Mixed evidence from the Manhattan Project; the main source (Physics Today) was not opened [Mission labs].

**L13. Give the lab a protected line to leadership and a host that absorbs risk.** [moderate] Rad Lab and MIT, Los Alamos and the University of California, JPL and Caltech (JPL moved to NASA on 3 Dec 1958). The Rad Lab's $106.8M is MIT's total OSRD contract value, not the lab's budget [Mission labs].

## 4. Commercialization and transition (the most repeated failure mode)

**L14. Design the hand-off before the invention matures.** [strong for PARC, moderate elsewhere]
- PARC: Xerox's Star targeted office-systems buyers at about $16,595 per seat (about $75,000 for a basic system, around 25,000 units); the IBM PC comparison is contested. Spin-offs succeeded when they built different business models than the copier model [Xerox PARC].
- Industrial labs: Corfam (withdrawn 1971), IBM's relational database and Menlo Park show different breaks in the chain [Industrial labs].
- DARPA: GAO found transition tracking unreliable; DARPA counted the biofouling program as a transition and GAO reclassified it because funding had ended; no common DOD definition exists. This strengthens the point [DARPA].

**L15. Keep research inside a business that is also the first customer, or pay for the missing link.** [moderate] Bell Labs; GE/DuPont tying science to existing platforms; PARC lacked an interface to the parent [Bell Labs; Industrial labs; Xerox PARC].

**L16. If spinning out, favor VC-style governance and room to experiment.** [moderate, one 35-venture study] Chesbrough (2003) finds a Xerox-insider CEO and Xerox's equity stake correlate with worse performance, and a higher VC share of board seats with better; it does not say "outside CEOs". Google X's insistence that graduates raise outside capital is consistent but weak evidence as a market test [Xerox PARC; Google X].

**L17. Treat IP policy as part of the innovation system.** [strong] The 1956 AT&T consent decree's royalty-free licensing is associated with about 17% more follow-on innovation by others (Watzinger et al.); this measures spillovers, not benefit to the lab. The patent counts (7,820 / 1.3% / 164) and the 17% come from search summaries and are not re-verified [Bell Labs; Literature].

**L18. Plan for reintegration and for the end state.** [weak-to-moderate] The Rad Lab closed with projects seeded into successors; Menlo Park ended because Edison's manufacturing moved (he "all but deserted" it by 1882) and West Orange also decayed, to 7 men by 1930. Successful X work has been reabsorbed into Google (Brain, Everyday Robots, Intrinsic) [Mission labs; Industrial labs; Google X].

## 5. Decline and durability

**L19. Shocks to ownership and funding, not lack of talent, track most decline stories.** [moderate, correlational] Bell Labs: 1984 divestiture, Lucent spin-off (30 Sep 1996), Alcatel merger (1 Dec 2006), Nokia (closed 3 Nov 2016). Nokia's 2026 research cuts (19 centers to 6) are reported by Light Reading; a claimed Core/Solutions merger is not in that article, and that split dates from 2021. Microsoft Research's Silicon Valley closure shows autonomy depends on the parent. Corporate science broadly receded (publications fell 1980-2006 while patent-measured value held) [Bell Labs; Institutes; Industrial labs].

**L20. Protect research-integrity systems as an organization shrinks.** [weak] The Schön case [Bell Labs]. The Nature 2006 abstract does not support a "budget cut pressure" claim.

**L21. Build institutions that outlast the founder.** [weak/inference] GE's lab outlived Whitney (recruited by Rice and Thomson; Steinmetz declined to run it). GE's 2017 closures were a cost cut under a new CEO, which is weak evidence about the model [Industrial labs].

**L22. Do not credit one agency with whole technology lineages, and do not copy surface features.** [moderate] Moderna and Siri show partial DARPA roles (Siri not re-verified); stealth was partial credit (1974-77 DARPA funding and Have Blue). ARPA-E's follow-on evidence is weaker than first reported: the Academies chapter gives 45 projects and over $1.25B, not "a quarter of about 500". Fraunhofer needs a dense SME base and a restructuring-capable state [DARPA; Institutes].

## 6. Corporate operating cultures (Pixar, Amazon, 3M, Toyota)

Source: `../centers/corporate-innovation-cultures-pixar-amazon-3m-toyota.md` (54 sources, each tagged opened, excerpt or snippet only; none graded strong).

**L23. Separate advice from authority in exploratory reviews.** [moderate] Pixar's Braintrust gives voice without veto and names one accountable owner; no outcome studies [Corporate cultures].

**L24. Write the customer outcome before building, and expect it to be insufficient.** [weak] Amazon's PR/FAQ and memos are testimonial evidence; the Fire Phone shows it is insufficient. Bezos says the Fire Phone failure fed the Echo [Corporate cultures].

**L25. Staff ventures with a dedicated, empowered leader.** [weak] Amazon's single-threaded teams trace, per the authors' site and Jassy's 2021 letter, to resource contention at scale, not to a finding that "leader quality beats team size". The "two-pizza" rule was not found in the 2016-2021 letters [Corporate cultures].

**L26. Keep options alive until learning justifies narrowing, when late change is costly.** [weak-to-moderate] Toyota set-based engineering; the SBCE evidence is thin and transfer beyond Toyota is difficult [Corporate cultures].

**L27. Protect exploratory slack, with sharing norms and platform reuse.** [weak] 3M's 15% time evidence is descriptive; every 3M-hosted page failed to open, so the rule's origin and conditions and its link to Post-it are unverified; the 1973 vs 1974 Post-it date conflict stays open [Corporate cultures].

**L28. Do not apply efficiency metrics unchanged to exploratory R&D.** [weak-to-moderate, causality contested] 3M's Six Sigma era saw new-product share fall while margins rose; but BusinessWeek says R&D funding was held flat under McNerney, not cut, and the "cut 25% / up 11%" figures were dropped [Corporate cultures].

**L29. Watch for drift after growth, acquisition or cost pressure; set portfolio guardrails.** [inference] Pixar post-Disney (Lasseter's 2018 exit, 2024 layoffs), Toyota [Corporate cultures].

**L30. Measure outcomes, not only inputs.** [weak] 3M's New Product Vitality Index; predictive validity not found [Corporate cultures].

## Where the evidence is thinnest

- No source gives a controlled comparison of any center against a counterfactual.
- Many headline figures are self-reported or not independently verified (X's kill rate, FRO results, Fraunhofer impact studies, DARPA headcount: CRS says 200+, Wikipedia 1,122).
- New models (FROs, ARIA, Arc, Wellcome Leap) have no outcome evidence yet; several were not re-verified [Institutes].
- Several key books were read only through excerpts or summaries (Gertner, Hounshell & Smith, Catmull, Bryar & Carr).
