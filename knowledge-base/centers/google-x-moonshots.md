## Snapshot

- **Founded:** January 2010 as Google X, by Sebastian Thrun and Yoky Matsuoka, beginning with the self-driving car project; renamed X when Alphabet formed in 2015 [12].
- **Parent and structure:** Alphabet became the public parent on 10 Aug 2015, with Pichai as Google CEO and also responsible for Other Bets [28] (wire report, not opened). The FY2025 10-K confirms X sits in Other Bets as an R&D-phase business, "X, our moonshot factory", alongside scaling businesses such as Waymo; Other Bets "operate as independent companies" [7].
- **Location:** Mountain View, California [12].
- **Size:** No source gives X headcount or budget. (unverified)
- **Funding model:** Funded from Alphabet. Other Bets is one segment and X is not broken out [7][8][9]. Operating loss and revenue from the 10-Ks: 2014 loss ~$1.94B on $327M revenue; 2015 loss ~$3.57B on $448M [9]; 2022 loss $4.636B [8]; 2023 $4.095B on $1,527M revenue; 2024 $4.444B on $1,648M; 2025 $7.515B on $1,537M [7]. The 2025 loss includes a $2.1B Q4 employee compensation charge for Waymo "based on estimated stock valuation", and the 10-K attributes the $3.1B year-on-year increase mainly to a valuation-based Waymo compensation charge [7]. So the 2025 jump is largely an accounting effect of Waymo's higher valuation, not only cash burn. The four years 2022-2025 sum to about $20.7B (my arithmetic from [7][8]).
- **Per-project losses:** Not disclosed. The 10-Ks do not break out Waymo, Verily, Wing or X [7][8], and the 2016 TechCrunch piece says spend on self-driving and Loon "isn't separately known" [9]. Blog figures for Waymo or Verily losses and a "$40B+ cumulative" loss could not be traced to a primary source and are dropped.
- **Status today (as of late Oct 2026):** Still operating and reported inside Other Bets [7]. Astro Teller (CEO; signs as "Captain of Moonshots") spoke at TechCrunch Disrupt on 27 Oct 2025 [3]. On 3 Aug 2026 X announced a partnership with Series X, an independent venture fund founded by Gideon Yu, to fund its strongest moonshots as new startups; X gives it first look at its pipeline and Alphabet "would retain a significant ownership stake" [2]. Spinouts named in the last 18 months: Taara, Chorus (AI supply chain), Anori (AI housing), and Verily Health (Series X led its March 2026 round); a Tidal aquaculture moonshot is cited as the kind of project needing this path [2].

## Operating model

- **Moonshot criteria:** A huge problem, a radical solution that would eliminate it, and breakthrough technology offering a glimmer of hope [3]. The Operating Manual adds: affecting millions or billions of people and a real chance of being achieved within 5 to 10 years [1]. The "10x not 10%" framing appears in a promotional consultancy blog with no sources [27] and was not found in the primary pages I opened.
- **Monkey first:** Teller: "You train the monkey: find out what is most likely to kill a project and go figure out that part first"; companies build the pedestal first because it feels like progress [5]. He frames the daily question as "what is the most likely reason this project won't make it?" [6].
- **Stage gates (from X's own manual) [1]:**
  1. Rapid Eval: a few weeks and a few thousand dollars, to find the biggest risks.
  2. Extended Investigations: a few months, prototypes and a techno-economic analysis.
  3. Foundry: about a year, teams usually under 10, expected to kill roughly half.
  4. Graduation: to a full X project with a general manager, or to an Alphabet company or independent startup.
  Teller said in 2016 that there is no fixed formal stage for timing decisions such as business plans [6], so the manual's stages describe the early funnel rather than a rigid process.
- **Milestones and budget:** Milestones target the biggest risks; retiring a major risk unlocks more budget or permission to grow the team [1]. Early tests get "a tiny amount of money" [3]. X manages a portfolio balanced across hardware/software, industries and time horizons, within budget limits [1].
- **Kill criteria in advance:** Confirmed by X: kill signals are metrics agreed at project start, and hitting them means "we should walk away from this project now"; the Foghorn team shelved its work after hitting them [1]. The podcast page says nothing on this [4].
- **Kill rate and hit rate:** See the provenance finding below. Verified numbers: X starts more than 100 efforts a year, about 2% reach exit five or six years later, and roughly 44% of total spend goes to projects that graduate [3]. Manual: hundreds of ideas last hours, dozens weeks, a handful months [1]. Teller estimated in 2016 that the project database holds "at least a hundred" killed efforts [6].
- **Rewarding killing:** The manual describes psychological safety so people can say something is not working, but no explicit reward or bonus for killing a project [1]. (bonus practice unverified)
- **Spin-out model:** Graduates become independent companies. Series X Capital (reported over $500M raised; legally required to invest only in X spinouts, per search snippets) is now the formal exit path, with Alphabet keeping a significant stake [2]. The Series X "first look" mechanism means it is not a fully arms-length market test.
- **Relationship to Google/Alphabet:** Since 2015 X is outside Google; the structure was meant to give non-core businesses independence [28]. Reabsorption has followed: Brain to DeepMind (2023), Everyday Robots into Google Research (2023), Intrinsic into Google (2026) [10][16][24].
- **Google's wider approach:** 20% time (about a day a week) is credited with Gmail, Maps, AdSense, Talk, but a 2013 report citing Quartz says it was "all but dead" [22]. The often-repeated Mayer 2006 "half of launches" statistic does not appear on that page (unverified).

## What worked (evidence-backed)

- **At least one very large outcome:** Waymo at $126B [14], and Teller's claim that Brain alone created value comparable to X's total cost [26]. Whether X's cumulative return is positive is not computable from public data; the 2022-2025 Other Bets losses alone are about $20.7B and exclude Waymo's own value (inference).
- **Cheap early failure (X claim):** small early budgets, with 44% of spend going to projects that graduate [3]. That implies most of the money goes to a small survivor set, not to the 98% (inference).
- **Pre-agreed kill signals** with a documented example (Foghorn) [1].
- **Outside capital as a signal:** Waymo's 2026 round had named outside leads [15]; but Alphabet funded most of it [7], and Verily only obtained a lead outside investor in 2026 [17].
- **Salvage:** Everyday Robots staff and technology moved to Research [10]; Loon's optical work reportedly became Taara.
- **Scale:** Wing's claims are company statements [13]; Waymo's 400,000+ weekly rides [14].

## Failure modes and criticisms

- **Losses:** About $3.57B in 2015 and $7.5B in 2025 for the whole Other Bets segment [7][9]; revenue is only ~$1.5B [7]. Only the 2025 spike is partly noncash [7].
- **Late kills:** Loon was killed nine years in and Makani after seven years at X or Alphabet [11][12]; Everyday Robots was shut after graduating, over its unclear mission [10]. These contradict the "kills early" shorthand.
- **Shutdowns and exits:** Makani 2020, Loon 2021, Glass enterprise 2023, Everyday Robots 2023, Verily devices 2025 [10][11][19][23].
- **Cost pressure:** Alphabet tightened X spending in 2016 under CFO Porat per Android Headlines (low-quality source: projects with 10+ year roadmaps to reach profitability in half the time) [25]; Loon's closure was tied to the same tightening [11].
- **Spin-out ambiguity:** "Graduated" units stayed Alphabet-controlled for years (Verily until 2026) [17]; Alphabet keeps stakes in Series X deals [2].
- **Self-reported rates:** 2%, 44% and the manual's funnel come from X and Teller [1][3].
- **Source disagreement:** The earlier draft cited a $110B Waymo valuation; every verified source says $126B [14][15]. Wing "deliveries" vs "flights" and a 150,000+ delivery count could not be traced; dropped.

## Lessons for modern operating models

1. Attack the riskiest assumption first ("monkey first"). [Evidence: moderate] Teller's description [5][6] and the manual's risk-based milestones [1]; no controlled outcome data.
2. Make killing cheap and normal, with pre-agreed kill signals. [Evidence: moderate] Documented in X's manual with an example [1]. The "98% killed early" statistic is not supported as stated (it is the complement of the 2% exit rate, which is measured years later) [3][4].
3. Force graduating ventures to raise outside capital as a market test. [Evidence: weak, downgraded from moderate] The Waymo round was mostly funded by Alphabet [7]; Series X invests only in X spinouts and Alphabet keeps large stakes [2]; Verily took 2015-2026 to get a controlling outside investor to a minority Alphabet stake [17]. The discipline is partial.
4. Judge a portfolio on power-law outcomes, not average return. [Evidence: weak] Waymo's valuation and Teller's Brain claim [14][26] versus ~$20.7B in losses over 2022-2025 [7][8]; 44% of spend goes to graduates [3]. No source computes net return.
5. Separate the lab from the core business organizationally. [Evidence: weak] The 2015 rationale is documented [28], but successful work has been reabsorbed (Brain 2023, Intrinsic 2026, Everyday Robots 2023) [10][16][24], suggesting separation is a stage rather than a permanent state (inference).
6. Reuse salvage assets when killing. [Evidence: weak] Everyday Robots [10]; Taara is reported but unverified.
7. Expect budget cycles to cut long-horizon bets; secure a funding path before downturns. [Evidence: moderate] Loon, Everyday Robots and Glass Enterprise all ended in cost-cutting periods [10][11][23]; 2016 tightening [25].
8. Unstructured time (20% time) generates products but decays without management support. [Evidence: weak] 2013 "all but dead" report [22]; the Mayer 2006 statistic is unverified.

## Open questions / contested claims

- Has X returned value net of costs? X's own spending is not broken out [7][8][9].
- Where did the "98% killed early" figure come from? Only a podcast episode summary (TechCrunch, 18 Nov 2025) by a producer states it; it is not a Teller quote on that page [4]. The Oct 2025 article says "2% hit rate", "about 2% reach exit five or six years later" and "kills all the bad ideas pretty early" [3]. Treat 98% as press shorthand for the complement of 2%.
- Was Brain an X project or Google Research's? Wikipedia and Teller's 2015 claim say it began in X and later moved to Google Research [12][26]; the 2023 merger article does not address origin [24]. Likely X origin; contested in popular accounts.
- Is a bonus for killing projects real? Not in the manual [1].
- Does Waymo's value reflect X's model or Alphabet's patience and capital? Alphabet funded most of the 2026 round [7].
- Wing cumulative deliveries (a late-2025 or 2026 milestone headline was seen but not opened) and X headcount remain unknown.
- Whether Intrinsic and Verily exits signal a retrenchment of X's spin-out model or a maturing of it is open (inference only).
