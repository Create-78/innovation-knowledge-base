## Snapshot

- **Founded:** 2010, originally "Google X"; renamed X when Alphabet formed in 2015 [21]. One result lists Sebastian Thrun and Yoky Matsuoka as founders; it began with the self-driving car project [21].
- **Parent and structure:** Alphabet announced on 10 Aug 2015 that Alphabet would replace Google Inc. as the public company, Page would be Alphabet CEO, Brin president and Pichai Google CEO, with Pichai also responsible for Alphabet's portfolio of "Other Bets" [15]. X sits among the non-core units reported in the Other Bets segment [11][15].
- **Location:** Mountain View, California [21].
- **Size:** Not confirmed from any source I could open. (unverified)
- **Funding model:** Funded from Alphabet. Alphabet reports Other Bets as one segment: FY2015 revenue $448M and operating loss $3.567B (FY2014: $327M and $1.942B) [11]; FY2024 revenue about $1.65B per Statista (2023: about $1.53B) [10]; the FY2024 10-K says the Other Bets operating loss rose $349M from 2023 to 2024, mainly employee compensation [8]. The absolute FY2024 loss was not shown in the excerpts I saw; one blog states about $4.4B and is unverified [12]. A 2016 headline reported Alphabet pressing X to tighten spending [24] (title only seen).
- **Status today (as of Oct 2026):** Still operating; CEO Astro Teller spoke for X at TechCrunch Disrupt in Oct 2025 [5]. Recent graduations reported include Taara (March 2025) and Heritable Agriculture (2025), per a secondary aggregator [23].

## Operating model

- **Moonshot criteria:** Teller defines a moonshot as the intersection of a huge problem, a radical solution that would make the problem go away, and breakthrough technology offering some hope [5]. Secondary summaries add the "10x, not 10%" framing [26 not opened; see 5].
- **Kill early, "monkey first":** Teller's analogy is to train the monkey to recite Shakespeare on a pedestal before building the pedestal, i.e. attack the hardest, most-likely-fatal part first [2][6]. X asks "what is the most likely reason this project won't make it?" [3].
- **Kill rate and hit rate:** TechCrunch reports X deliberately kills 98% of ideas early [4]; Teller cites a "2% hit rate" which he calls acceptable [5]. Early ideas get small amounts of money to try to prove them wrong, and a "no" is fine [5].
- **Kill criteria in advance:** The phrase appears in a 2026 podcast description (promotional) saying written criteria prevent "zombie" projects [4]; I found no primary X text confirming it. X also publishes a "Moonshot Factory Operating Manual" post [1] that I could not open.
- **Rewarding killing projects, stage gates, rotation, staffing:** Commonly cited in the literature, but I could not confirm these from any source I saw. A claim that a bad moonshot costs $50M was in a search-result title only [unverified]. (unverified)
- **Relationship to Google/Alphabet:** Since 2015 X is outside Google, under Alphabet; non-core businesses moved under Alphabet and were meant to be independent with their own brands [15]. Google retains Search, Maps, Android, YouTube and ads [15].
- **Spin-out model:** Mature projects "graduate" into separate companies, increasingly pushed toward outside investors; projects that cannot attract them are more likely to be shut [13][12]. A dedicated Series X Capital fund backing X spin-outs (reported over $500M raised) is claimed by a secondary source [23].
- **Google's wider approach:** 20% time (about a day a week on own projects) is credited with Gmail, Maps, AdSense, Talk; Marissa Mayer said in 2006 that about half of recent launches came from it, but later reports said it had faded [17].

## What worked (evidence-backed)

- **At least one very large outcome:** Waymo's $126B valuation and $27B raised [14] dwarfs the cumulative losses if the Bloomberg figure holds; whether it exceeds the whole Other Bets loss history is not computed in any source I saw (inference).
- **Commercial scale for Wing:** thousands of weekly deliveries and sub-19-minute fulfillment claims (company statement) [19].
- **Early failure was cheap relative to late failure (claim by X):** Teller says small early budgets let X learn "no" [5]; no independent audit seen.
- **Outside capital as a discipline:** Waymo's 2026 round attracted Dragoneer, DST, Sequoia and others [14], which independently validates it.
- **Pivots salvage value:** Taara grew from Loon's optical communication work [21]; Everyday Robots people moved to Google Research [13].
- **20% time:** product outcomes credited by Google's own president-level statements [17].

## Failure modes and criticisms

- **Losses:** Other Bets lost $3.567B in 2015 on $448M revenue [11]; FY2024 revenue was about $1.65B, with the loss still growing ($349M more than 2023) [8][10]. The $40B+ cumulative figure from one blog is unverified [12].
- **Shutdowns:** Makani (about 2020), Loon (2021, nine years in) [12], Everyday Robots (2023, with layoffs) [13], Verily device exit (2025) [20]. Loon is cited by Bloomberg as a failed project whose last balloons were grounded [22].
- **Glass:** the consumer product was a public failure; its end was widely noted in 2023 [25].
- **Cost pressure:** Alphabet tightened X spending as early as 2016 [24], and later cuts reached Everyday Robots [13].
- **Spin-out ambiguity:** "graduated" units such as Verily and Mineral remain Alphabet-controlled; Alphabet reportedly sought to sell or spin off Verily [20][23].
- **Self-reported rates:** The 2% hit rate and 98% kill rate come from Teller and press summaries [4][5], not independent measurement.
- **Source disagreement:** Waymo valuation $126B (Bloomberg) vs about $110B (eWeek) [14]; Wing "deliveries" vs "flights" [19]; Statista's own 2023 revenue figure was inconsistent in presentation [10].

## Lessons for modern operating models

1. Attack the riskiest assumption first ("monkey first") before building infrastructure. [Evidence: moderate] Teller's own framing and journalism describe it [2][3][6]; no controlled outcome data.
2. Make killing cheap and normal: small early budgets and an explicit "no" outcome. [Evidence: moderate] Stated by Teller [5]; the 98%/2% numbers are self-reported [4].
3. Force graduating ventures to raise outside capital as a market test. [Evidence: moderate] Waymo's 2026 round and the push to outside investors [13][14]; counterexample Verily's difficulties [20].
4. Judge a portfolio on power-law outcomes, not average return. [Evidence: weak] Inference from Waymo's valuation versus segment losses [8][11][14]; no source computes net portfolio return.
5. Separate the lab from the core business organizationally (Alphabet holding structure) to protect both. [Evidence: weak] The 2015 rationale is documented [15]; benefit is not demonstrated. Brain's later merger into DeepMind [16] suggests that AI work eventually needed to return near the core (inference).
6. Reuse salvage assets when killing (Loon to Taara, Everyday Robots people to Research). [Evidence: weak] Anecdotal [13][21].
7. Expect budget cycles to cut long-horizon bets; secure a funding path before downturns. [Evidence: moderate] Everyday Robots 2023 [13], X spending pressure 2016 [24].
8. Unstructured time (20% time) generates products but decays without management support. [Evidence: weak] Mayer's 2006 claim and later reports of its fading [17]; the Brain origin is from a weak source [18].

## Open questions / contested claims

- Has X returned value net of costs? X's own spending is not broken out; Other Bets is aggregated [8][11], so no source gives X's cumulative cost or return.
- Is "kill criteria set a year ahead" X policy? Only a promotional podcast blurb says so [4].
- Are stage-gate details, the bonus for killing projects, rotation and headcount accurate as popularly told? Not verified here.
- Was Google Brain an X project or a Google Research one? My sources say it began in 20% time and later sat under Google Research [16][18]; X's role is disputed in popular accounts.
- Does Waymo's value reflect X's model or Google's data/compute and Alphabet's patience? Unresolved.
- The Other Bets absolute FY2024 loss and cumulative losses need the 10-K segment note (the SEC page could not be opened) [8].
- Dates for Taara, Intrinsic, Mineral and Series X Capital rest on a secondary aggregator [23].
